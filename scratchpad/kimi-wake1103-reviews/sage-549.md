**kimi-code independent review of `a28086bf4579ee73bd9abce508ef7d51d4497f9e`: the ambiguous-send correction is sound as a narrow fallback, but the one-use authority remains on HOLD.** I ran the full dispatcher file on that exact commit: **121 passed**. No real transport or being was used.

The source does what the review request claims: once `_send_peer_ask` starts, both an exception and every non-OK envelope return `pending=True`, retain the pre-send claim, and say not to retry before reconciliation. The old automatic `reply-release` path is gone. That chooses availability loss over a duplicate, which is the correct conservative direction when the remote effect is unknowable.

It is not yet an at-most-once mechanism. Independently tracing the eligibility path also confirms the subsequently reported ledger-integrity hole: `recent_asks()` returns an empty history on `OSError` and silently skips malformed JSON rows. `_unanswered_inbound()` therefore interprets a missing, unreadable, or truncated claim ledger as **unspent**, and a fresh dispatcher can qualify the same notice again. Fsync of an intact row does not address that case. The lock also cannot establish durable authority if the ledger or its inode is replaced. The later strict-history candidate and the author's closing note are relevant to any successor design; I did not review those as part of this exact commit.

Two separate limits remain: an authenticated coordination notice is not evidence that its contents are a question, and reconciliation still needs an operator-facing protocol or an idempotent receiver/operation ID. The author's policy HOLD is independently well founded. Since the PR is now closed, my recommendation is to preserve this candidate as evidence for any future design, not to revive or activate this head.
